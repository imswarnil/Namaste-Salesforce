Training navigation

The rail that runs down every training and lesson page — and the answer to the rule you set: the section you are in stays open, every other one is closed.

How the behaviour works

Each section is a <details> sharing one name attribute. That is a native HTML exclusive accordion: the browser itself keeps exactly one open and closes the previous when another is opened. No JavaScript, no flash on load, and it still behaves if the script never runs. A browser without exclusive-details support just allows more than one open — which degrades correctly rather than breaking.

What is deliberately NOT in it

No lesson count on the section row. The expanded list already shows it, and the number was the noisiest thing in the rail. No training name or progress — those belong to the page, not to the navigation, and repeating them above every rail was the single biggest source of clutter. They live in the sub bar instead.

No horizontal scroll, ever. Every text cell is min-width: 0 with truncation and the rail clips its x axis — a sidebar you have to scroll sideways is a sidebar you cannot read.

Lesson type icons

Each lesson row carries what KIND it is — video, exercise, quiz or article. The number says where you are in the section; the icon says what you are about to open, which is the thing you actually scan for.

It has to FIT

Every level of the rail — group, list, row, text — is min-width: 0, so the title is what truncates and the duration is never clipped off the edge. The indent under a section is deliberately small, because every millimetre there comes off the lesson title, which is the only thing in the row worth reading.

Section icons

The section's own image is its icon: the tag image first (set once, shared everywhere the section appears), then the post's feature image, then ONE shared fallback glyph. The same glyph for every section, so a missing image reads as “section” rather than as a different kind of thing. The open section's icon goes solid.

Motion

The house rule, applied consistently: 120ms, ease-out, colour and background only — plus a 2px slide toward the reader on hover. That transform is the one allowed here, because it reads as “this is the row under your cursor” rather than as decoration. All of it is off under prefers-reduced-motion.

A window column

The rail runs from the site header to the bottom of the viewport and scrolls inside itself, so its progress footer sits at the bottom of the SCREEN rather than at the bottom of a list nobody scrolled to. That is why it is a flex column, not a grid — the last child has to be pushable to the end.

Room to scan

Rows are taller than the type strictly needs. A rail is SCANNED, not read, and a scannable list needs air between its items far more than it needs to fit one more row on screen. Sections carry that further: half a rem of padding either side of the rule, so it reads as a separation rather than as a line.

Where things sit

Back to training is the FIRST thing in the rail, above the search — a back-path buried under the navigation is a back-path nobody finds. Progress is the LAST, pinned to the bottom: it is a status readout you glance at, not somewhere you navigate to, so it stays in view while a long section scrolls past.

Sections are ruled, not spaced

A hairline separates one section from the next, and an open section is bracketed top and bottom so its lessons read as belonging to it. Gaps alone would not survive a collapsed accordion — which is most of the time — where the whole rail becomes one undifferentiated list. Getting from one section to the next is the pager's job at the foot of the lesson, not a row inside the navigation.

A title can never break out

Every ancestor of the rail is min-width: 0 and every text cell truncates, so a long section or lesson name shortens rather than widening its container.

States

.is-current is the lesson you are on — solid brand, the same mark the docs rail uses. .is-done swaps the number for a tick and steps the title back.

All three states read in ONE place — the marker slot. Normally it holds the lesson number; .is-done swaps it for a tick, .is-locked for a lock. The eye learns one position instead of three, and the row never has to move or resize to carry the message.

.is-locked additionally DIMS the row to 50%. Dimming is the honest signal in a scanned list: everything you can act on sits at full strength, everything you cannot is quieter — no warning colour, because “you need an account” is not an error. Hover restores it completely, since the row is still a link (it goes to the lesson page, which is where the sign-up is) and must never look disabled under the cursor. The same rule applies wherever a locked lesson appears: .ns-list__item.is-locked on a section page and .ns-track-card__lesson.is-locked in a track card preview.

What you will NOT find anywhere: a count of how many lessons are free, or a badge naming a tier. A tally like “1 free” is noise at best and looks broken at worst, and a section is a section — not a price. The lock on the individual row is the whole access story.

Spacing

The rail spends the foundation's tokens — --space-row-sm and --space-inline for rows, --space-gap-sm for gaps and either side of the section rules. It had drifted onto its own invented scale (0.2, 0.35, 0.45, 0.5, 0.65, 0.75rem …), which is exactly how a component stops looking like the rest of the theme.
